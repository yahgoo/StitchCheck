Research evidence-backed pain points experienced by budget travellers, with a
focus on problems that occur before, during, and after low-cost airline travel.

Goal:
Build a source base for a hackathon idea-selection process. Find credible,
recent sources that explain real user pain, frequency or severity, current
workarounds, and gaps in existing travel products.

Prioritize:
- Missed connections, delays, cancellations, and irregular operations.
- Hidden fees, baggage constraints, fare changes, refund friction, and
  confusing low-cost carrier rules.
- Time pressure, cross-border uncertainty, fragmented information, and
  difficulty comparing recovery options.
- Safety, accessibility, trust, and decision fatigue under travel disruption.
- Evidence from consumer bodies, transport regulators, airlines, travel
  research, customer surveys, reputable journalism, and academic research.

For each finding, identify:
1. Target budget-traveller segment.
2. Trigger moment.
3. Specific pain point.
4. Existing workaround and why it is insufficient.
5. Evidence of severity, frequency, or economic impact.
6. Source URL, publisher, and publication date.

Exclude:
- Generic travel-planning advice.
- Unsupported blog opinions.
- Ideas or product recommendations.
- Claims without a citable primary or reputable secondary source.

Produce a structured research report with:
- Top pain points ranked by evidence strength and urgency.
- A source list grouped by pain point.
- Contradictions, gaps, and assumptions needing validation.

Do not recommend a hackathon idea. This research will be combined with three
independent idea reports from Kimi, Z.ai, and ChatGPT before NotebookLM selects
one candidate.
